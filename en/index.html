Responsive people before passive viewing

What a baby is already noticing deserves a real person who names it, pauses, waits, and responds. A screen is not a substitute for a relationship.

See evidence ↗

No child-side lessons for 6–17 months

This stage is for parent guidance: read it, put the device down, and use printed cards, books, and real objects. Co-viewing is off by default at 18–23 months; 24–36 months can also stay screen-free.

See evidence ↗

Back-and-forth matters more than more input

Notice what the child is attending to, name it, pause, and respond to a look, gesture, movement, or sound. Then return the idea to daily life.

See evidence ↗

Natural Chinese is the main path; English is optional

Bilingualism is not a race. Each language needs real, sufficient, natural input; unfamiliar English or recorded audio cannot replace rich conversation.

See evidence ↗

Math starts with quantity, space, and patterns in life

Touch one, say one, then name the total. Put things in, take them out, sort, and arrange. No flashcards and no correctness score.

Theme index
01Black, White & Red+

Look and play with printed cards, familiar faces, and a floor mat. No rapid card changes, shape tests, or forced tummy-time sessions.

  • Move one printed card slowly and pause to watch the baby's response.
  • Respond to a look, smile, or sound without asking the baby to imitate.
  • Offer short tummy-time moments while awake; stop when the baby is tired.
02Hello, Baby+

Share real family photos, facial features, and gentle songs. Use the people and names familiar to your family, without expecting words or a consistent response to baby's name.

  • Prefer real faces or family photos and use familiar names.
  • Tell the baby what you are doing while dressing and name one feature at a time.
  • Learn the song during the day, then sing one line with the screen off.
03Peekaboo+

Hide a little and discover together. Briefly cover the caregiver's face, partly hide a large toy, and share a safe mirror. Looking without searching or smiling is welcome.

  • Cover only the grown-up's face; keep the baby's nose and mouth clear.
  • Begin with a large toy partly visible; the grown-up may help reveal it.
  • Share a shatter-resistant rounded baby mirror without testing self-recognition.
04Animal Sounds+

Take turns talking and singing with printed animal cards and a caregiver's voice. Repeat familiar short songs and use screen-free humming in a gentle bedtime routine.

  • Use one card at a time, name it softly, and pause for a response.
  • Lift the matching printed card while you sing; do not play background audio.
  • Learn lullabies during the day, then hum with the screen off at bedtime.

01Cause & Effect+

Use bubbles, boxes, and cups to explore appearing, disappearing, in, and out.

  • Use real bubbles and large toys to notice changes caused by an action.

02Clap and Wave+

Practice imitation, gestures, and waiting through face-to-face play and songs.

  • Model the gesture first, then pause for the baby's own response.
  • Make hello and goodbye part of the same small family ritual.

03Yummy Food+

Hear food names, more, all gone, and simple number talk during safe meals.

  • Use foods the baby already eats; do not make the meal longer.
  • Say two only when both pieces are visible, and say all gone when the plate is empty.
  • Respect a closed mouth, turning away, or pushing away as a stop signal.
04Things at Home+

Follow your baby's attention and connect home objects, cards, and songs to daily life.

  • Follow the baby's attention first, then name the real object.
  • Use the goodnight card book during the day; keep bedtime screen-free.
  • Support the body during rocking or movement and keep the motion small and slow.

Things to notice

  • ✓Place first words in care, play, and going-out routines.

Theme index
01Animal Friends+

Use animal cards and short stories for names, sounds, and turn-taking.

  • Point to animals with large cards or safe toys.
  • After copying a sound, pause and wait for the baby's response.
  • Keep stories gentle and pretend-based; do not create fear or separation.
02My Body+

Name body parts during care and songs while respecting body boundaries.

  • Name body parts during bathing and dressing.
  • Let the grown-up model first; watching or doing one small part is enough.
  • Use slow songs and gentle movement without pulling or forcing touch.
03Let's Go+

Notice vehicles safely and explore rolling and speed with a toy.

  • Notice real vehicles from far away, away from roads, wheels, and doors.
  • Use a low, steady ramp with a large toy vehicle.
  • Learn the song first, then sing and push a real toy with the screen off.
04One and Many+

Talk about one, many, one, and two with visible everyday objects.

  • Use large safe objects, not small foods, to talk about quantity.
  • Touch one and say one, then name the total.
  • Use songs for shared rhythm, not for testing number recognition.
05Getting Dressed+

Connect clothing words with going out and hand-washing routines.

  • Show clothing while dressing and invite a hand or foot movement.
  • Use a routine card to preview socks, shoes, and a hat.
  • Learn the handwashing song first; stay within reach near water.
06Outdoors+

Hear nature words and move slowly during safe walks and weather noticing.

  • Follow the baby's gaze and name a tree, flower, bird, or cloud.
  • Listen to rain indoors first; avoid standing water and unsafe surfaces.
  • Imitate a tree, bird, or rabbit without asking for jumping or balance.

01Colors+

Hear red, yellow, and blue with clothes, printed cards, and scribbles.

  • Look for one color at home.
  • Put matching-color socks together.
  • Offer free scribbling without asking for color naming or a rainbow.
02Shapes+

Find round edges, match shapes, and build a low tower.

  • Notice outlines with round objects and shape cards.
  • Model a shape-matching toy without forcing it into place.
  • Build a low tower on the floor; try again if it falls.
031-2-3+

Count one to three visible objects, then say how many.

  • Touch one and say one, then name the total.
  • Count spoons and shoes, not small foods.
  • Play stop-and-go gently, without elimination or breath-holding.
04Feelings+

Hear feeling words and experience support and asking for help.

  • Look at feeling cards and practise asking for help when calm.
  • When a real feeling arrives, put the device down and protect safety first.
  • Respect the child's wish for a hug or breathing space while staying close.
05Clean & Healthy+

Children join care routines while grown-ups handle hygiene and safety.

  • Adults help brush twice a day and use only a rice-sized amount of toothpaste.
  • Stay within arm's reach around water.
  • Wash with soap for at least 20 seconds, then rinse and dry.
06Big & Small+

Compare matching objects, nest cups, and gently reach.

  • Compare two objects from the same kind at a time.
  • Begin by nesting two large cups, then add a third if interested.
  • Use sitting or supported standing; do not ask for jumping or tiptoeing.

Theme index
01Sorting+

Notice colors and kinds, and group similar things.

  • Sort large objects with clear main colors into red and blue.
  • Model one object, then wait for the child's attempt.
  • Say what feature you are sorting by; perfect accuracy is not the goal.
02Count to 5+

Touch one as you say one number and explore small groups.

  • Begin counting with one or two large objects.
  • Name the total after counting; do not test numeral recognition.
  • Use counting songs as shared play, not a quiz.
03Animal Homes+

Notice animal features and read about a panda eating, climbing, and resting.

  • Say swims in water and flies in the sky; do not say birds live in the sky.
  • Read animal books together and never approach or feed wild animals.
  • Pandas can climb trees; keep the child's pretend play on the ground.
04Taking Turns+

Listen to each other's feelings and practice brief turns.

  • Model taking turns without forcing a child to give up a treasured toy.
  • Ask how the child feels without sorting feelings into right and wrong.
  • Use polite words inside real help and repair.
05Where Is It?+

Find the cat's position and explore up and down through movement.

  • Use dolls and open boxes to say in, out, up, and down.
  • Do one step first, then invite the child to give the grown-up a direction.
  • Stay off furniture, out of closed boxes, and away from climbing play.
06Weather+

Notice the weather outside and choose what to take along.

  • Notice local weather; a book is fine when snow is not part of daily life.
  • Choose clothing for the real temperature, not only the weather icon.
  • A rain song is a wish, not a way to stop the rain.

Theme index
01Patterns+

Start with alternating pairs, then explore groups of three together.

  • Use socks and towels to make two complete repeats.
  • Take turns clapping and stamping softly; sitting is an option.
  • A three-part pattern is optional; return to two alternating items if needed.
02Count to 10+

Adults model larger sets while children explore small sets hands-on.

  • Touch one and say one, then name the total; do not test numeral reading.
  • Start with one to three large objects; adults can support four or five.
  • Line up same-sized objects one-to-one, then talk about more and fewer.
03I Can Do It+

Break familiar routines into small steps, with room to try and ask for help.

  • Sit securely while trying one clothing step; an adult checks comfort and safety.
  • Use a two-step plan: first this, then that.
  • Do not rush toileting; clean carefully after toilet or diaper care.
04Off to Preschool+

Explore preschool routines, goodbyes, requests for help, and kind words.

  • Use dolls to practise goodbye and a promised pickup without creating real separation.
  • Let the child say what feels worrying and name a trusted teacher for help.
  • Name real objects; follow the preschool's own pickup and belongings rules.
05Healthy Me+

Explore food variety, safe movement, and screen-free bedtime routines.

  • Offer a varied everyday diet; keep sweets occasional and never force a clean plate.
  • Lower the movement challenge, steady the body, and do not turn balance into a test.
  • Learn bedtime songs during the day and keep bedtime screen-free.
06Nature Changes+

Observe specific plants and local weather rather than treating pictures as universal rules.

  • Adults handle seeds and soil while children observe a real seedling.
  • Talk about day and night at clear times; the moon can also appear during the day.
  • Look at the same tree repeatedly and allow local seasons to differ.

Run it at home

One small server,devices kept in the house

The living-room screen does not need to know the wider internet. A Mac, NAS, or small computer serves the player on the home network; a phone opens the parent area only when needed.

01

Home network

The server listens on 4310 by default. The TV, iPad, and parent area use the same family network.

02

Data stays at home

The server stores family data in local SQLite; offline records stay on the current device.

03

Six-digit pairing

The player requests a six-digit pairing code that is valid for 10 minutes; the device token is not printed on the page.

04

No server required to start

The player ships with the sprout.core content pack, so a family can try offline before connecting a home server.

Will this make children watch more screens?+

It is not designed to extend viewing. Ages 6–17 months get adult guidance only; co-viewing is off by default at 18–23 months; ages 24–36 months can also stay fully offline. Every lesson has a natural ending and an offline next step.

What about babies younger than six months?+

The core pack starts at 6 months and has no child-side playback path for younger babies. Face-to-face response, floor play, singing, books, and professional child-health guidance come first.

Where does family data live?+

When connected to a server, family data lives in local SQLite on the device running @sprout/server. Offline records stay on the current device.

Why are there no narration recordings in the repository?+

The public repository does not ship macOS system voices because their license does not allow public redistribution. Without pre-recorded audio, the page uses the browser Web Speech API; a product build can use local or licensed TTS.

Is it free, and can it be used commercially?+

Code is MIT. Course content is for personal, family, and non-commercial use under the repository's CC BY-NC 4.0 terms; commercial use requires written author permission. Third-party assets keep their own licenses.

Is this medical advice?+

No. Sprout is not a medical device, treatment tool, or developmental diagnostic. Milestones are for everyday observation and conversations with professionals. Consult a pediatric or child-health professional when concerns persist or skills regress.
